Just go. It's saved.

Loop notices when you start a run, a walk or a ride and tracks it with just your iPhone. When you stop, it saves the workout, names it and turns it into a card worth posting. Around the world, small circles of people keep each other going, and nobody is ranked against anybody.

    Cards worth posting

    Six looks in two tones, sized for a 9:16 story. One tap opens Instagram with the card ready. Try your own photo on the second one.

    A card for every city

    Run in a big city and the card knows it. Each city gets its landmark drawn in the same single line as your route, its name in its own script, and the city's coordinates, never yours. Anywhere else gets the map card.

    The first eight of about 40 for launch: the big running cities, then wherever people use Loop most.

    It starts by itself

    You shouldn't have to press start. Your iPhone already knows when you walk, run or ride: a low-power motion chip keeps that record all day for almost no battery. Loop reads it, and turns on GPS only when a real workout begins.

    Every walk, run and ride

    Read from the motion chip's own record: time, steps, distance and floors. They show up in You without a tap, at zero extra battery. No route, because GPS stays off.

    Runs and rides with the route needs Always

    When you move off and the motion chip says running or riding, GPS comes on. The first minutes come from your steps, so nothing is missed. It stops when you stop or get in a car, then saves and names itself.

    With an Apple Watch

    The Watch already notices workouts on its own. Loop picks them up from Apple Health with the route and heart rate. Swims and gym sessions count properly too.

    WalksShort walks are logged without GPS. A walk turns on GPS only after about 15 minutes away from home or work, so errands don't drain the battery.
    Not a workout?Swipe it away. Loop learns the places and patterns you don't want counted.
    Start buttonStill there. Tap it before a run if you want the route from the first step.

    Together, not versus

    No leaderboards and nobody to beat. Loop puts you in a few small circles, and each circle moves as one: who showed up today, and how close the group is to its week. Nobody has to start a group or organise it. The app does that.

    Friends, anywhere

    The people you invite, in Dubai, London or Toronto. One circle, one shared week, whatever the time zone.

    Nearby

    Six to twelve people who move in your part of town, formed by the app and refreshed each month. Made-up names like "Kite 41", never real ones.

    Same hour

    People around the world who go out at the same time of day as you: dawn, lunch or late night. Someone is always out when you are.

    What a circle sees

    Showed up todayA filled dot for each person who moved today. Your own dot is blue.
    The circle's weekOne shared count, like 14 of 20 outings. Everyone adds to it, nobody owns it.
    Cheers and nudgesOne tap. A quiet friend gets a nudge, not a ranking.
    Never shownDistances, paces, times, routes, places and any kind of ranking.

    The live map

    Open Loop and the first thing you see is a live map of people moving: a glow wherever people are out walking, running or riding right now. It is built from anonymous counts for areas a few kilometres wide, and an area only lights up once at least five people are in it, so no one can be picked out.

    It does the boring bits

    Small things that add up to an app you don't have to think about.

    Where the smarts come from

    Mostly the phone's own sensors and plain maths. One small language model writes the names, and it runs on your iPhone. No cloud AI and no ChatGPT or Claude calls, so nothing about your workouts is sent anywhere to be analysed.

    No AI model

    Sensors and simple rules

    Walk or run
    Motion sensors and step rate tell walking from running every few seconds, so one workout holds both.
    Auto-pause
    GPS speed drops to zero and the phone stops moving.
    Device check
    Before you start, Loop asks iOS which sensors and devices are there for that activity.
    Walk home
    A long slow stretch after you finished running gets offered for trimming.
    Hide your home
    The spot most of your workouts start from.
    Highlights
    Fastest km, longest run stretch, streaks. Plain maths on your own history.
    On-device language model

    Apple's model, on your iPhone

    On newer iPhones it can write the name. Your iPhone 13 Pro doesn't have it, so Loop names workouts with rules, led by the place. A small model shipped inside the app is an experiment for later.

    Light on battery

    Battery is a feature. Loop spends it only while you are actually working out, and we measure it on your phone instead of guessing.

    A normal dayUnder 1% is the target. Motion history and low-power location wakes only. No GPS, no network.
    During a workoutGPS once a second, paused by itself when you stand still. Nothing is drawn while the phone is locked.
    NetworkOne small upload when a workout ends. The live map loads only while Loop is open. Nudges arrive as notifications, never by checking in the background.
    MeasuredApple's MetricKit reports Loop's own battery use every day. We check it after every build.

    A server that knows nothing about you

    Circles and the live map need a small server. It is built so it never holds personal data: no names, no phone numbers, no emails, no contacts, no routes and no precise places. If it leaked tomorrow, nobody could learn who you are or where you live.

    On the server

    • A random ID your phone makes up. No sign-up, no email, no password.
    • A made-up handle like "Kite 41".
    • Which circles you are in.
    • Days you showed up. A yes for a day, nothing more.
    • Live map counts per area of a few kilometres, with no ID attached.
    • A notification token so a nudge can reach your phone.

    Never leaves your phone

    • Your contacts. You invite people with a link you send yourself.
    • Names. Your phone labels friends from your own contacts. The server only sees random IDs.
    • Routes, distances, paces and times.
    • Where you live, work or start.
    • Health data. It stays in Apple Health.

    One trade-off. Loop can't tell you which of your contacts already use it, because that means sending their phone numbers to the server, and even scrambled numbers still count as personal data. So you invite with a link, and the app does everything after that: circles, nudges and the shared week.

    The plan

    A native iPhone app in SwiftUI plus one small server. Each step ships to your phone as soon as it works, and nothing personal ever reaches the server.
